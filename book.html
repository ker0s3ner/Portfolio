Overview

The Spectral Theorem: A Concise Guide to Operators, Spectra, and Projection-Valued Measures is a 53-page, 13-chapter mathematical monograph by Benas Petronis. It builds the language of spectral theory from the ground up: inner-product spaces and induced norms, orthogonality and projections, completeness, and Hilbert spaces, followed by bounded operators, adjoints and spectra.

The book proves the finite-dimensional spectral theorem, then introduces projection-valued measures and states the theorem for bounded self-adjoint operators as an integral representation, with the continuous functional calculus, the normal-operator version and spectral projections. Applications and computational approximation follow.

The second half moves to unbounded self-adjoint operators and the unbounded spectral theorem, compact operators and spectral asymptotics, spectral types, multiplicity and dynamics, Stone's theorem on one-parameter groups, symmetric operators and self-adjoint extensions via deficiency indices, and perturbation theory with bound states.

Central idea: The spectral theorem connects the geometry of Hilbert spaces with the structure of self-adjoint operators, moving from familiar matrix diagonalization to the measure-theoretic language required in infinite-dimensional analysis.
